Step 1

Documents we need

One form covers every kind of case. Have the relevant documents ready before you start — you can upload them as you go. If something is unavailable, say so in the form and explain why; we may be able to help you get it.

In every case

  • All immigration documents in your client’s possession — for example a green card, work permit, USCIS paperwork, or the visa stamp in a passport. If a document exists but you cannot get hold of it, describe it.

Criminal cases

  • Fingerprint RAP sheet. Repository inquiries are generally insufficient.
  • All accusatory instruments, including supporting affidavits and police reports.

Family court, divorce and other non-criminal cases

  • Any draft or filed pleadings — especially anything concerning family offenses, orders of protection, or alleged acts of misconduct. If none exist yet, tell us what is going to be alleged.

Step 3

Before you receive RIAC advice

Please read these now rather than waiting to hear from us. Several of them are time-sensitive.

In every case

  • If your client is subject to an order of protection, immediately review the advisory on orders of protection and foreign-born clients.
  • If there is an ICE detainer, or your client is likely to be arrested by ICE — in court, on release from jail, or at home — immediately review the advisories on mandatory immigration detention and ICE presence in New York courtrooms.
  • Do not use a court interpreter outside of court — that conversation is not privileged. You have access to telephonic interpretation: see the advisory on court interpreters, or contact us if you need help arranging it.
  • Do not ask your client to contact RIAC directly. We are your legal expert, not your client’s immigration attorney.
  • Advise your client of their right to refuse to speak with ICE, even to confirm their name. Ask us for the “Know Your Rights with ICE” flyers in English, Spanish or other languages.

Criminal cases

  • Do not accept any plea, or enter any diversion program — even a seemingly excellent one, including an up-front plea that will be vacated. See the advisory on diversion and up-front pleas.
  • Do not advise your client to accept an ACD until you have reviewed the ACD advisory with them.
